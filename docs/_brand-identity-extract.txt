CHAPTER ONE
Executive Summary
Phoenix Vapers is not entering a growth market. It is inheriting a corrected
one — a market in which trust, compliance, and engineering discipline
have become the only durable sources of advantage. This book builds the
brand for that reality.
or a decade the British vaping industry rewarded speed over substance. Brightly coloured
disposable devices, sweet packaging, and disposable-income pricing built enormous
volume and almost no loyalty. That era is over. On 1 June 2025 the sale of single-use
disposable vapes became illegal across all four nations of the United Kingdom. On 29 April 2026
the Tobacco and Vapes Act received Royal Assent, bringing an advertising ban, a retail licensing
regime, and enforceable point-of-sale powers. From 1 October 2026 a Vaping Products Duty of
£2.20 per ten millilitres applies to every e-liquid sold, nicotine-bearing or not. The market has
been forcibly matured. What remains is a category that must now compete on the things Phoenix
Vapers has quietly done well for years.
This is the strategic opening the brand has been waiting for. Phoenix Vapers already
manufactures in the United Kingdom, analyses its e-liquids through professional chemists, holds
ISO 9001 certification for its quality management, and has every product independently risk-
assessed by EL-Science. In the old market these were invisible virtues. In the new market they
are precisely what a licensed, tax-registered, advertising-restricted trade demands. The task of
this brand system is not to invent a story. It is to make the truth of the business legible, premium,
and impossible to confuse with the disposable culture the country has just rejected.
The strategic position in one sentence
Phoenix Vapers is positioned as the standard of trust in British vaping: an engineering-led,
compliance-first manufacturer and retailer that treats a regulated nicotine product with the
seriousness it deserves. Every subsequent decision in this book — the retention and disciplining
of the green, the adoption of a neutral grotesque and a technical mono, the restraint of the
packaging, the sobriety of the voice — exists to make that single position felt before it is read.

CHAPTER TWO
About Phoenix Vapers
A Peterborough manufacturer and retailer with a rare combination of
assets: it makes what it sells, tests what it makes, and operates across the
full chain from formulation to shelf.
Phoenix Vapers Limited operates from Peterborough as a vertically engaged business rather than
a reseller. It formulates and produces e-liquids in the United Kingdom, retails through its own e-
commerce platform and physical stores, supplies the trade at wholesale, and maintains its own
hardware, coil, and accessory ranges alongside proprietary e-liquid lines. This breadth is
unusual. Most vaping businesses occupy a single link in the chain — they either make liquid, or
import hardware, or run shops. Phoenix Vapers spans formulation, quality assurance, retail, and
distribution, and that span is the material foundation on which the brand is built.
The four operating identities
The organisation should be understood as four businesses sharing one standard. As a
manufacturer, it develops e-liquid recipes and produces them under controlled conditions, with
proprietary lines that give it identity and margin independent of third-party brands. As a retailer,
it sells directly to the adult consumer through a website and a store network, owning the
relationship rather than renting it. As a wholesaler, it supplies other retailers, extending the
standard beyond its own shelves. And as an emerging technology platform, it holds the data,
logistics, and compliance infrastructure that the licensed future of the category will require. The
brand must be legible across all four without fragmenting.
The evidence already in the business
Three assets deserve particular emphasis because they will carry disproportionate strategic
weight in the chapters that follow. The first is UK manufacture with in-house chemical analysis:
products are produced domestically and examined by professional chemists rather than
accepted on a supplier's word. The second is ISO 9001 certification, an internationally
recognised standard for quality management that signals systematic process control rather than
ad hoc effort. The third is independent risk assessment through EL-Science, a specialist that
has invested substantially in product safety science and whose consumer-facing assessments
Phoenix Vapers makes available in its stores.
WHY THIS MATTERS NOW
In a licensed, tax-registered market with mandatory product registration, these credentials stop
being reassurances and start being requirements. Phoenix Vapers already meets a bar the rest
of the market is being forced to reach. The brand's job is to make that head-start unmistakable.

CHAPTER THREE
The Industry & the UK Market
The United Kingdom has, in eighteen months, rewritten the rules of
vaping more comprehensively than in the previous decade.
Understanding that new rulebook is the precondition for every brand
decision that follows.
aping in Britain sits at an unusual intersection. It is simultaneously a public-health
instrument — the country's most effective smoking-cessation aid — and a consumer
category under intensifying regulatory constraint. The brand cannot pretend the second
reality away, nor should it. The companies that will lead the licensed era are those that treat
regulation not as a burden to be survived but as the terrain on which trust is won. Phoenix Vapers
is built to lead precisely there.
The three settled facts
Three regulatory realities now define the market and are unlikely to reverse. First, the disposable
ban: since 1 June 2025 it has been illegal to sell single-use vapes anywhere in the UK, a measure
driven by environmental waste — millions of devices discarded weekly — and by youth-access
concern. The category has migrated to refillable and prefilled pod systems, exactly the
responsible, reusable formats Phoenix Vapers already prioritises. Second, the Tobacco and
Vapes Act 2026, which received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026, introducing a vaping advertising
and sponsorship ban, a retail licensing scheme, mandatory product registration, point-of-sale
display powers, and on-the-spot fines for underage sales. Third, the Vaping Products Duty: from
1 October 2026, £2.20 per ten millilitres applies to all e-liquid regardless of nicotine content, with
registration having opened in April 2026.

What the advertising ban does to branding
The forthcoming prohibition on vaping advertising and sponsorship is the single most
consequential fact for this document. When a category can no longer buy attention, the brand
itself — the packaging on the shelf, the fascia of the shop, the experience of the website, the
tone of a customer email — becomes the entire marketing surface. There is no campaign to
compensate for a weak identity. The identity is the campaign. This is why the investment
represented by this book is not cosmetic: in an advertising-restricted market, the brand system is
the primary commercial asset the company owns.
DESIGN IMPLICATION
Every pound previously available to shout must now be earned through restraint. The most
valuable square centimetres Phoenix Vapers owns are the label, the fascia, and the interface.
This book treats each as prime media.
The duty and the flight to quality
The Vaping Products Duty compresses the market's historic advantage — cheapness — and
rewards its historic weakness — quality. When every ten millilitres carries the same £2.20
charge, the relative premium of a well-made, well-tested, well-packaged product shrinks. A
consumer paying duty on everything has less reason to accept an inferior product to save a few
pence. This is a structural tailwind for a manufacturer whose entire proposition is that its liquids
are made properly and tested independently. The brand should never mention tax; it should
simply be the obvious choice once tax has flattened the field.

CHAPTER FOUR
Consumer Behaviour
The adult vaper of 2026 is not the impulse buyer of 2021. They are more
informed, more sceptical, more cost-aware, and — after a decade of hype —
quietly exhausted by the category's noise.
The disposable era trained a generation of consumers to treat vaping as fast-moving and
disposable in every sense: cheap to buy, easy to abandon, loyal to nothing. The correction has
produced a different customer. Forced onto refillable and pod systems, they must now make
considered choices about hardware, coils, and liquid. They must understand nicotine strength,
PG/VG ratio, and flavour longevity. They are, in short, becoming users rather than consumers —
and users reward competence.

CHAPTER FIVE
Competitive Landscape & SWOT
The competition is not other vape shops. It is the visual and behavioural
clutter of the entire category — and the empty premium centre that no
British vaping brand has yet credibly claimed.
The UK vaping market is crowded at two poles and vacant in the middle. At one pole sit the mass
brands and disposable-descended pod systems — high volume, loud packaging, price-led, now
scrambling to look responsible. At the other pole sit hobbyist and sub-ohm specialists —
technical, enthusiast-facing, visually chaotic, speaking to a narrow expert audience. Between
them lies the position no one occupies with conviction: the premium, trustworthy, adult
mainstream — the vaper who wants neither a toy nor a rig, but a well-made product from a
company that behaves like a grown-up.

CHAPTER SIX
Positioning
Positioning is the single most important decision a brand makes, because
it determines every decision after it. Phoenix Vapers takes the one position
the market has left open and that the business is uniquely equipped to
defend.
position is not a slogan or a look. It is the specific place a brand occupies in the mind of
a customer relative to every alternative — the answer to the question "why this one?"
that forms before the customer is consciously aware of asking it. A strong position is
true, distinctive, and defensible. It must be honest to the business, different from competitors,
and difficult to copy. Phoenix Vapers has all three available to it, provided it has the discipline to
choose narrowly and hold the line.
The positioning statement
POSITIONING STATEMENT
For the adult who has chosen to vape and refuses to be careless about it, Phoenix
Vapers is the British vaping company that engineers trust into every product —
because it makes what it sells, tests what it makes, and treats a regulated product
with the seriousness it deserves.
Read the statement closely, because each clause is load-bearing. "For the adult who has chosen
to vape and refuses to be careless about it" defines the audience by attitude rather than
demographic — the considered vaper, not the impulse buyer. "The British vaping company that
engineers trust" claims the category benefit — trust — and attributes it to a method —
engineering — rather than to assertion. "Makes what it sells, tests what it makes" supplies the
proof. "Treats a regulated product with the seriousness it deserves" converts the constraint of
regulation into a signal of respect. The statement is defensible because a competitor cannot
claim it without also building the manufacturing, testing, and compliance infrastructure that
stands behind it.

CHAPTER SEVEN
Purpose, Vision & Mission
Three statements, three time horizons. Purpose is why the company exists
at all. Vision is the future it is working toward. Mission is what it does
every day to get there.
These three are frequently confused and often reduced to interchangeable marketing lines. They
are not interchangeable. Together they form a hierarchy of intent that should cascade into every
plan, every hire, and every design decision. Stated well, they are internal instruments before they
are external claims — the compass the organisation steers by, not the flag it waves.
PURPOSE · WHY WE EXIST
To bring integrity to a category that has too often lacked it — so that
choosing to vape need never mean choosing carelessly.
The purpose reaches beyond commerce without abandoning it. It acknowledges the category's
real failings and positions Phoenix Vapers as the corrective. It is deliberately durable: it will
remain true whether the company sells liquids, hardware, or the technology platforms of a
licensed future.
VISION · WHERE WE ARE GOING
To be recognised as the most trusted vaping company in the United
Kingdom — the standard against which the responsible market measures
itself.
The vision is a destination, not a description. "Most trusted" is a measurable ambition — in
prompted and unprompted brand trust, in retention, in wholesale advocacy. "The standard
against which the market measures itself" sets the bar higher still: not merely to be trusted, but to
define what trustworthiness in the category looks like.
MISSION · WHAT WE DO DAILY
To engineer, test, and supply vaping products of uncompromising quality,
and to make the evidence of that quality plain to everyone we serve.
The mission is operational and testable. It commits the company not only to quality but to the
transparency of quality — the second clause is what distinguishes Phoenix Vapers from any
competitor content to be good in private. Making the evidence plain is a design brief as much as
an operational one, and it recurs throughout the visual and verbal systems that follow.

CHAPTER NINE
Brand DNA, Personality & Archetype
If the brand were a person, who would it be? The answer governs how it
speaks, what it wears, and how it makes people feel — and it must be
consistent across every encounter.
Brands are understood by people the way people are understood by people: through a small
number of consistent traits inferred from behaviour. A brand with a clear, stable personality is
easier to trust because it is predictable, and predictability is the foundation of trust. The following
model defines Phoenix Vapers' character so precisely that a copywriter, a designer, and a
customer-service agent who have never met will nonetheless produce work that feels like the
same company.
Brand DNA
At the core sit four traits that cannot be compromised without changing what the brand
fundamentally is: Precise, Responsible, Understated, Assured. These are the genetic markers.
Every expression of the brand should be testable against them — if a piece of work is imprecise,
careless, loud, or anxious, it is off-brand regardless of how attractive it may be.
The archetype
Drawing on the archetypal framework that underpins much of modern brand theory, Phoenix
Vapers is primarily The Sage, with a supporting register of The Creator. The Sage seeks truth,
values expertise, and earns influence through knowledge and honesty rather than charm or
dominance. It is the archetype of the trusted expert — the pharmacist, the engineer, the analyst
— and it is exactly right for a company whose entire proposition is competence you can rely on.
The Creator support brings the engineering pride: the satisfaction of making something properly,
to specification, without shortcuts.

Brand Story & Narrative
A brand story is not history. It is the meaningful shape given to history —
the reason the company's past explains why it is the right choice now.
he Phoenix is one of the oldest symbols in human culture, and it has been trivialised by
the vaping category into a shorthand for fire and smoke. This book reclaims it for its true
meaning, which has nothing to do with flame. The Phoenix is the emblem of renewal
through discipline — of something better rising from what came before, deliberately and by
design. That is the brand's story, and it maps precisely onto both the company and its market.
The narrative in three movements
What came before. A category that grew too fast on products made too carelessly, that treated
adults like children and the environment like an afterthought, and that has now been corrected by
regulation and by its own excesses. The disposable era is the ash.
The renewal. From that correction rises a different kind of vaping company — one that makes
rather than merely sells, tests rather than assumes, and treats the customer as an adult. Not a
rejection of vaping, but its maturation. The Phoenix does not escape the fire; it is defined by what
it becomes after it.
The standard set. Phoenix Vapers does not simply participate in the renewed market. It sets the
standard for it — the reference point the responsible industry measures itself against. The story
ends not with survival but with leadership.
NARRATIVE DISCIPLINE
The Phoenix is expressed as ascent, renewal, and rigour — never as literal fire, smoke, or flame.
This is a firm rule of the visual system that follows. The symbol earns its gravity precisely by
refusing the cliché the category expects of it.
The elevator narrative
Compressed to a single spoken paragraph, for use by anyone representing the company:
"Phoenix Vapers is a British vaping company built on a simple idea — that a regulated product

is the whole business, and it is the whole brand."

CHAPTER NINETEEN
Colour Accessibility
Accessibility is not a constraint on the palette. It is a discipline that makes
the palette work — and it produces one rule that every designer and
developer must internalise.
The brand green is vivid, but vividness and contrast are not the same thing. Measured against
the WCAG contrast standard, #08AB5E on white reaches only 3.0:1 — sufficient for large text
and interface components, but below the 4.5:1 required for normal body text. This is not a flaw to
hide; it is a specification to design around. The system resolves it cleanly with a single governing
rule.
THE GOVERNING ACCESSIBILITY RULE
Never set body text in Phoenix Green on a light background. For green text on light, use Deep
Pine #05633A (7.36:1 — passes AAA). Reserve Phoenix Green for fills, large display type,
interface accents, and use on dark backgrounds, where it reaches 6.2:1 on Ink Green.

CHAPTER TWENTY-EIGHT
Governance & Evolution
A brand system is only as strong as its enforcement. This chapter defines
how the identity is protected, who protects it, and how it is permitted to
change without losing itself.
Ownership & approval
The brand requires a single accountable owner — a brand guardian or custodian — with the
authority to approve or reject any application of the identity. Consistency is not achieved by good
intentions distributed across many hands; it is achieved by clear ownership and a defined
approval path. External partners work from this book and from the master asset library, and their
output is signed off against both before release.
ARTEFACT APPROVAL
New packaging or product identity Brand guardian + compliance sign-off
Campaign or environmental graphics Brand guardian
Digital product changes to tokens Brand guardian + engineering
Routine collateral on template Delegated, within system rules
Any use of the logo by a third party Brand guardian, in writing
Asset management
Master artwork — logos, fonts, tokens, templates, this document — lives in a single, versioned,
access-controlled library. No one recreates an asset that already exists; no one works from a file
pulled off a website or a colleague's desktop. Each asset is versioned, and the current version is
the only permitted one. This is the operational backbone of consistency, and it is treated with the
same rigour as the product's own quality management.
Evolution roadmap
A brand must be stable to be trusted and alive to stay relevant. Phoenix Vapers resolves this
tension by distinguishing the fixed core — position, values, mark, green, type system — from the
flexible edge — campaigns, photography, seasonal expression, new applications. The core
changes rarely and only through formal review; the edge evolves continuously within the system.
The likely evolution over the coming decade tracks the market: deeper investment in the digital
and data platform as licensing matures, extension of the standard through wholesale, and

Executive Statement
Everything in this book reduces to one idea, and the idea is worth stating

he United Kingdom vaping market has been forced to grow up. The disposable culture is

this book is built to create.
